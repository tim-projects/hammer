perform_move (tasks_ai/utils.py) renames a task by writing the destination and then deleting the source: atomic_write(new_filepath, task) at line 332, artifact moves at 342-351, then shutil.rmtree(filepath_str) at 353. Between 332 and 353 the task folder exists in two state folders on disk.

Readers that scan every state folder during that window: task_service.find_task (scans all STATE_FOLDERS and returns the first match), tasks list (builds rows from every folder), and the guard (GUARD_STATE_DUPLICATE exists precisely for one id in two state folders). None of them distinguishes a task that is genuinely duplicated from one mid-move. Consequences for simultaneous work: the other agent's gate fails with a state-duplicate reject, its 'active task' resolution can pick the stale copy, and its checkpoint can then commit from the wrong folder. The window is milliseconds per move but moves are one of the most frequent store operations.

The store lock from task 336 is held only by commit_task_paths, which runs AFTER the physical move - so the lock does not cover the window, and readers take no lock at all.

Fix direction (in increasing order of completeness): (1) hold store_lock across the ENTIRE physical move so writers serialize and the window can only be observed by a reader; (2) make readers deterministic when both folders exist - find_task should prefer the folder matching the record's own St metadata, and list should emit one row; (3) have the guard dedupe same-id folders by St consistency instead of rejecting outright. (1) alone leaves readers racy, so the fix is (1)+(2), with (3) as the guard-side guard rail. Test by constructing the both-folders state directly in a temp store and asserting find_task/list/guard behavior, plus a threaded test where a reader runs during a locked move.
